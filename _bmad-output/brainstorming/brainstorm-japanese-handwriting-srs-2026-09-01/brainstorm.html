Ink & Interval

A tablet-and-stylus companion for practicing Japanese handwriting — kana and kanji, reviewed on a spaced-repetition schedule, drawn with a real pen instead of tapped with a thumb.

Mode: autonomous Status: complete Updated: 1 Sept 2026, 21:04 Techniques: 3

The goal: replace finger-typing on phone SRS apps with pen-based writing practice — a simple, honest, end-to-end MVP for local testing, borrowing the best traits of the apps already out there without dragging in their scope.

02 / 03 — Technique

要 Job to Be Done

What the app is really hired to do

Nineteen jobs, laddered from the core hire down to the smaller reasons the user keeps coming back.

Core job

Build muscle memory for correct stroke order — so grading must check the stroke sequence, not just the final shape.

J02

Let me self-grade without a teacher, via auto stroke-order and shape scoring with an instant visual diff overlay.

J03

Don't waste my limited daily study time on things I already know — SRS must actively hide mastered characters and surface only due ones.

J04

Let me practice with real ink feel — pressure/tilt-sensitive rendering prioritized over feature breadth in the MVP.

J05

Build a form-writing habit like a diary — each session is a page written today, reinforcing a physical-journal metaphor.

J06

Confirm I actually recall it, not just recognize it — the default review always requires producing the character, never multiple choice.

J07

Connect the shape I write to how it's read and what it means — each card links character, reading, and meaning in one review.

J08

Let me learn words, not just isolated kanji — seed vocab includes common compound words using kanji already learned.

J09

Let me see my improvement, via a simple per-character mastery meter (box level 0–5) visible on a home dashboard.

J10

Don't let me lose progress from missing a day — SRS backs off the interval on a missed day instead of silently piling up overdue cards.

J11

Give me the tactile pleasure of a pen so I don't quit — stylus latency and ink smoothing are the emotional hook worth investing in early.

J12

Don't grade me harshly for imperfect handwriting — scoring should be tolerance-based approximate shape match, not pixel-exact.

J13

Let me practice specifically what I write badly, by tracking per-stroke error hotspots and re-surfacing recurring-error characters more often.

J14

Let me feel proud of a physical artifact — export or save a rendered image of the day's writing page.

J15

Let me practice even without a network — the app works fully offline for portable tablet sessions.

J16

Let me trust the SRS is scheduling correctly, via a simple transparent due-date display per card, so it doesn't feel like a black box.

J17

Let me correct false negatives — allow overriding an auto-grade when the user knows they actually got it right.

J18

Let me not re-learn stroke order for characters that share components, by grouping characters by shared radicals so learning transfers.

J19

Let me warm up before the hard stuff, by ordering the daily queue easiest/most-overdue first, to build confidence before harder new cards.

03 / 03 — Technique

削 One Feature Only

Finding the one unbelievably-good thing

Four candidate MVPs, two alternate framings, and a cutting-room floor — kept on the left, cut on the right.

Candidate AWrite-to-recall SRS flashcards for kana only (46 hiragana + 46 katakana) — the smallest scope that still exercises the full pipeline end-to-end.
Candidate BA stroke-order-checked kanji writing drill with no SRS scheduling yet — pure practice mode only.
Candidate CA daily due-cards queue with pen input and self-graded pass/fail — content-agnostic, no automatic stroke-order validation.
Candidate DA digital genkouyoushi notebook — a beautiful, low-latency grid-paper writing surface with save/export, no grading or SRS at all, to validate ink feel first.
Alternate EJust the pen canvas with save-as-image and nothing else — a pure sketch app that validates the tablet/stylus stack before touching SRS.
Alternate FJust a due-cards counter plus a flip card (front prompt, back answer image), no drawing evaluation at all — the cheapest possible SRS loop, where drawing is only for the user's own eyes.
Chosen synthesis

Combine C's self-graded due-queue with D's high-quality pen canvas, deferring automatic stroke-order recognition (from B) entirely to v2.

“The one unbelievably-good thing should be whatever is technically riskiest — stylus latency and ink rendering — since that's the actual differentiator versus existing phone apps. De-risk that first, even if the scheduling logic starts crude.”

Kept

  • Local persistence, so progress is never lost.
  • The simplest defensible due-cards algorithm — SM-2-lite or Leitner.
  • The pen canvas, tuned for ink feel over feature breadth.
  • A plain self-graded “did I get it right?” tap.
  • Self-grading avoids needing OCR / stroke-matching ML entirely.
  • Seed content: a fixed hardcoded list of the 46 hiragana, to prove the loop fastest.

Cut, for now

  • Mnemonics
  • Stroke-order animation
  • Multi-device sync
  • Vocab / compound words
  • Separate reading / meaning quiz modes
  • Streaks or gamification
  • Cloud account

Concrete MVP definition

46 hiragana. A flashcard prompt (romaji or audio) is shown. The user writes on the canvas. The user self-marks correct or incorrect. SM-2-lite reschedules the card. Progress is saved locally (SQLite / localStorage). Repeat tomorrow.

結 Synthesis

Three techniques, one trunk stroke

Thread 1 — from Trait Transfer

Procreate-style pressure ink, the emotional hook, and the technically-riskiest thing worth de-risking first are the same idea wearing three hats: ink rendering is the actual product, not a nice-to-have.

Thread 2 — from Job to Be Done

Self-graded pass/fail (Leitner / SM-2-lite, no OCR) resolves the tension between “check real stroke order” and “defer ML recognition” — the user becomes the grader in v1, satisfying the job without the engineering cost.

Thread 3 — from One Feature Only

The genkouyoushi grid canvas and the “digital notebook” one-feature candidate reinforce the same canvas and background design — one surface, not two.

Three threads, stamped into one direction.

Chosen MVP direction: 46 hiragana only, SM-2-lite / Leitner scheduling, a self-graded pen canvas on a genkouyoushi grid, local persistence — no OCR or stroke-order ML, no mnemonics, gamification, or sync in v1.
